\chapter{Detailed Models of Overcontact Binary Stars}\label{chap:close_contact}

\epigraph{There are more things in Heaven and Earth, Horatio, than are dreamt of in your philosophy.}{Hamlet}

As previously discussed, stars undergoing mass transfer will generally experience \gls{rlof} during their lifetimes. In a binary where both stars are undergoing mass transfer, both stars may undergo \gls{rlof} simultaneously, resulting in a contact binary system in which both stars physically touch. Contact binaries are not uncommon -- they form one in every approximately 100 main-sequence stars \citep{Rucinski:1998} and 39 in every 100 binary systems \citep{Green:2023}.

The evolution is also complex. Sufficiently close models may overflow their $\mathcal{L}_2$ points, or create mergers on exceptionally short timescales -- typically $P_\text{orb}\lesssim\SI{2}{\day}$, but periods as low as $P_\text{orb}=\SI{85}{\minute}$ \citep{El-badry:2022} are possible in theory or $\sim\SI{167}{\minute}$ in practice \citep{Koen:2022}.
