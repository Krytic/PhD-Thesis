\chapter{Helium Rich Mass Transfer}\label{chap:he_mass_transfer}

\epigraph{We, all of us, are what happens when a primordial mixture of hydrogen and helium evolves for so long that it begins to ask where it came from.}{Jill Tarter}

\lettrine[lines=3]{S}{ince the force between two} objects due to gravity scales like $F\sim\frac{1}{r_\text{sep}}$, another question naturally arises during the consideration of contact binaries. For sufficiently close binaries, one can imagine that mass transfer through \gls{rlof} could become reasonably strong, stripping the hydrogen envelope of a main sequence (or shortly post-main sequence) star and exposing the helium-enriched core underneath. If this stripping does not cause the star to retreat within its Roche lobe, then this core material would continue to be accreted, creating helium-enriched matter streams accreting onto its companion.

As discussed in \autoref{sec:binary_evolution}, the presence of a companion measurably alters the evolution of a star. Typically this manifests as allowing matter to be exchanged between the components or the gravity of one star warping the photosphere of the other. In this chapter we focus on the first instance, where mass transfer occurs between two stars. We consider a binary system with \gls{zams} masses of \SI[parse-numbers=false]{30+10}{\msun}, and an initial period of $10^{0.6}\approx$ \SI{3.98}{\day}. We specifically explore this case as a case study on the effect of Helium enriched mass transfer, and in \autoref{chap:population_properties} we will explore the effect that this has on a grid of close binaries.

The general background for this section remains as elaborated in \autoref{chap:theoretical_background}, however our parameterisation requires us to delinate between two cases: that when variable composition accretion is enabled (referred to hereafter as ``variable accretion'') and the case where it is disabled (hereafter ``non-variable accretion''). These correspond to setting the parameter IVARACC in \autoref{chap:param_config} to 1 or 0 respectively.

\subsection{Pre-interaction}

The pre-interaction phase of both stars is identical in both cases. They evolve as a fully detached binary for \SI{4.51}{\mega\year}, with the donor completing its main sequence evolution. Hydrogen is exhausted in the core, and a helium core develops. In both cases, as the donor begins to cross the Hertzsprung gap, it inflates to fill its Roche lobe, causing matter to begin to be exchanged via \gls{rlof} and kick-starting the pre-contact phase.

\subsection{Pre-contact}

When the donor swells to fill its Roche lobe, it begins to efficiently transfer material to its companion. The increased drag causes the orbit to become hardened; shrinking by approximately 20 per cent. The accretor begins to accrete material, bleeding angular momentum into the system and causing the accretor to spin up. As the contact phase approaches, it approaches 15 per cent of the critical rotation rate, as shown in \autoref{fig:stellar_profiles}.

\section{Atypical abundance profiles}

Because the stars are quite close together and have a mass ratio of $\nicefrac{1}{3}$, when \gls{rlof} starts it is quite voracious, and becomes even more so during the contact phase. The donor star sheds its hydrogen-rich surface and material from the helium-rich core is exposed, which too becomes part of the accreted material. The material stream becomes increasingly helium-rich, depositing this material on top of a hydrogen-rich surface, causing the accretor to have an atypical abundance profile, as shown pictorally in \autoref{fig:composition_profile_evolution}. The net effect is that there is a helium-rich surface sitting atop a hydrogen-rich layer sitting atop a helium-rich core. At this stage thermohaline mixing is projected to occur, as there is an unstable composition gradient (owing to helium having a higher mean molecular weight than hydrogen).

\section{Comparison between variable and non-variable accretion}

In \autoref{fig:hrd_comparison_var_novarr} we display a comparative HR diagram between the cases of variable and non-variable accretion. In each case an \SI[parse-numbers=false]{30+10}{\msun} binary was evolved with the only difference between the two being whether material was allowed to have variable composition accretion or the standard accretion prescription. In the bottom panel only the companion is shown, as the donor has identical evolution in both cases.

The first and most striking conclusion that we can draw from this result is that the companion is, in the variable accretion case, hotter and more luminous than the non-variable case.
